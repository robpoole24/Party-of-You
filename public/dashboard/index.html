🗃️ Get Your Voter File

State voter files are public record. Most states provide them free or for a small processing fee. You generally need to be a registered candidate to request one.

How to Request
  1. Register your campaign committee with your state
  2. Contact your Secretary of State's elections division
  3. Submit a voter data request form with your committee ID
  4. Pay any processing fee (usually $0-25)
  5. Upload the file here — we parse any format
What You'll Get
✓ Name, address, age for every registered voter
✓ Party registration (where available)
✓ Voting history (which elections, not who they voted for)
✓ Phone numbers (in some states)
✗ Email addresses (not in voter files)

💡 Email Best Practices

📬
Subject Lines
Keep it under 50 characters. Use your community's name. Avoid spam words like "free" or "urgent."
⏰
Best Times to Send
Tuesday–Thursday, 10am–2pm local time. Avoid Mondays and Fridays. Never send Friday evening.
📝
Email Length
Under 300 words for updates. Under 500 for newsletters. Always one clear call to action.
📊
What Good Looks Like
Political emails average 20–25% open rate. Above 30% is excellent. Click rates of 3–5% are healthy.

Highest Priority
The single biggest gap in the platform. Right now we can show you federal and state races — but most independent candidates win their first election at the local level: city council, county board, school board, water district. BallotReady covers 130,000+ local races across all 50 states with continuously updated data sourced directly from election authorities. Here's exactly what we'd get:

One row per race — every combination of position, election day, and race type (primary, general, runoff, unexpired term). Continuously updated from official election sources.
✓
Filing deadlines — exact start/end dates, including separate deadlines for incumbents vs. challengers and major vs. minor party candidates
✓
Position details — official name, district/ward/region breakdown, number of seats, term length, and election frequency
✓
Race type flags — primary, runoff, unexpired term, blanket primary, majority-vote primary rules by state
✓
Partisan type — partisan, nonpartisan, or partisan-for-primary-only (critical for independent candidates)

✓
Level classification — federal / state / regional / county / city / local, enabling precise filtering
🏛️ Dataset 2 — Incumbent & Officeholder Data
One row per officeholder term. Current and historical officials from federal down to local — who holds the seat, how long, and how to reach them.
✓
Full name + party — first, middle, last, suffix, nickname, and party as it appeared on the ballot
✓
Term dates with precision — exact start/end plus a specificity flag (day/month/year accuracy) so you know how solid the data is
✓
Open seat detection — vacancy flag plus off-cycle flag for special elections and mid-term appointments
✓
Contact information — office phone, district phone, email, and mailing address per officeholder
✓
Web presence — government website, Wikipedia, LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Facebook, Instagram links
✓
Appointed vs. elected — flag distinguishes elected winners from appointed replacements
✓
Full term history — every prior officeholder per seat, enabling incumbency research and term-limit tracking

OpenSecrets — Opponent Money Tracking
Who funds your opponent? OpenSecrets tracks every dollar in federal and state races — PAC money, corporate donors, dark money flows. Knowing who's bankrolling the incumbent is campaign gold. It lets you draw a direct contrast: "My opponent takes money from X. I don't take a dime from them." Currently we use FEC data which covers federal races but OpenSecrets enriches it with industry categorization and context.
📋 Includes: Donor breakdowns, industry categorization, dark money tracking 🔄 Updated: As FEC filings come in

L2 / TargetSmart — Enhanced Voter File
Game Changer
State voter files tell you who's registered. Enhanced voter files tell you who actually votes, what they buy, what they read, and how likely they are to support a progressive candidate. L2 and TargetSmart append commercial consumer data to voter records — this is what every major party campaign uses to target their door knocking, phone banking, and mail. Without this you're knocking on every door. With it, you knock on the right ones.
📋 Includes: Modeled turnout scores, partisan scores, consumer overlays 💡 Note: Raw state voter files are always free via our voter file guide
