What We Stand For

Core Beliefs

Every candidate who joins this platform agrees to these principles. They are not negotiable. They are not open to corporate interpretation. They are what we mean when we say we work for working people.

1

Healthcare is a human right

Every person deserves access to healthcare regardless of their income, employment status, or zip code. We support universal, publicly funded healthcare and oppose any legislation that cuts, privatizes, or restricts access to Medicaid, Medicare, or public health programs.

2

Workers come before shareholders

We support a living wage, the right to organize, overtime protections, and safe working conditions. We oppose union-busting, right-to-work laws designed to destroy collective bargaining, and the continued transfer of wealth from workers to the investor class.

3

Housing is a necessity, not an investment vehicle

Every person deserves a stable, affordable place to live. We support tenant protections, social housing, and restrictions on speculative real estate practices that price working families out of their own communities.

4

Public education must be fully funded

Every child deserves a quality public education. We oppose the defunding and privatization of public schools through voucher programs and charters designed to extract taxpayer money for private profit. We support canceling student debt and making public college tuition-free.

5

The climate crisis demands immediate action

We accept the scientific consensus on climate change without qualification. We support aggressive investment in clean energy, a just transition for fossil fuel workers, and holding the corporations most responsible for the crisis accountable.

6

Civil rights are non-negotiable

We stand unconditionally for the rights of Black Americans, Indigenous peoples, immigrants, women, LGBTQ+ people, people with disabilities, and every other marginalized community. We will not trade their rights for votes or compromise them to appear electable.

7

Democracy belongs to people, not donors

We oppose Citizens United and the entire architecture of dark money in politics. We support public campaign financing, ranked choice voting, automatic voter registration, and every measure that makes our government more responsive to citizens and less responsive to corporations.

8

Wealthy individuals and corporations must pay their share

The concentration of wealth at the top is not an accident — it is the result of policy decisions made by politicians funded by the people who benefit. We support progressive taxation, closing loopholes that allow billionaires to pay lower rates than their employees, and investing that revenue in public goods.

Who This Is Not For

The Line in the Sand

Most political platforms tell you what they're for. We're also going to be clear about what we won't allow — because a platform that claims to serve working people while welcoming those who exploit them is not a platform, it's a con.

  • Candidates who accept corporate PAC money or dark money contributions. If a corporation is funding your campaign, you work for them — not for the people in your district. This is a hard disqualification.
  • Candidates who oppose abortion rights or bodily autonomy. The right to make decisions about one's own body is fundamental. There is no version of working-class politics that requires women to surrender control over their lives.
  • Candidates who oppose LGBTQ+ rights or use our platform to target marginalized communities. Full stop.
  • Candidates who support privatizing Social Security, Medicare, or Medicaid. These programs keep tens of millions of Americans out of poverty.
  • Self-described conservatives running as "independents" to circumvent accountability. We are a left-populist platform. We are not a home for rebranded Republican politics.
  • Corporate Democrats who use progressive language while voting for corporate interests. The problem we're solving is not just Republicans. It is an entire political culture that treats corporate donors as the real constituency.

On Costs — The Honest Version

Free Infrastructure.
Shared When It Isn't.

This platform will never charge you a subscription. There are no premium tiers, no feature paywalls, no commission on donations. The tools we build are yours.

But we're going to be honest with you about something the fine print usually hides: some of the most valuable data in politics — comprehensive local race databases, verified election deadlines, hyperlocal candidate filings — is owned by third-party organizations that charge for API access. Ballotpedia, for instance, charges thousands of dollars per year for the data that would make the open seat tracker genuinely complete at the school board and city council level.

Here is exactly how we handle that:

The Commons Model

When a paid data source would meaningfully improve a feature for every candidate on the platform, we will tell you openly: what it costs, what it unlocks, and how many candidates contributing would make it sustainable. The first candidates who fund access unlock it permanently — for every current and future candidate, in every district, at every level.

  • ✓ We name the cost before asking for it. No surprises. You'll see exactly what a data source costs, broken down to what each contributing candidate's share would be.
  • ✓ Every feature works without it. Paid data sources enhance features — they don't gate them. A candidate in a state where we have no Ballotpedia access still gets FEC data, Census demographics, OpenStates legislative data, and live polling. The free version is genuinely useful.
  • ✓ Once unlocked, it stays unlocked. A candidate in Wisconsin who contributes toward Ballotpedia access is directly funding better tools for a candidate in Georgia they'll never meet. That's not a side effect of the model — that's the point of it.
  • ✓ We publish our costs. Every dollar that goes toward platform infrastructure is publicly disclosed. We are not building a black box that extracts value from grassroots campaigns. We are building shared infrastructure — and you should be able to see exactly what it costs to run it.

This is the same principle behind every union, every co-op, and every mutual aid network that has ever worked: when working people pool resources, they build something none of them could afford alone. Party of You is political infrastructure built on that logic.

Our Commitment

What We Promise You

We ask you to take a stand. Here's what we commit to in return.

🔒

Free — With One Honest Exception

The platform, our tools, and every data source we can provide for free will always be free. Some premium third-party data feeds — like Ballotpedia's local race database — carry a cost we pass through transparently. But here's how that works: the first candidates who fund access unlock it for every candidate on the platform permanently. You're not paying for yourself. You're paying it into the commons.

📡

No Ads, No Data Sales

We will never run ads. We will never sell your data or your supporters' data. We make no money from your campaign.

💸

Your Money Stays Yours

Every dollar donated to your campaign belongs to your campaign. We do not take a cut. We are not a fundraising platform extracting fees.

🗂️

Your Data Belongs to You

If you ever leave the platform, you get a full export of every piece of data associated with your campaign within 14 days. No hostage data.

⚖️

Transparent Enforcement

If we believe a candidate has violated these principles, we follow a documented process: formal notice, opportunity to respond, and an appeal window before any account action.

🏗️

We Build With You

This platform is built in public. Candidates can see what we're working on, request features, and provide feedback that shapes what gets built next.
